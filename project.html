Why bother sorting it right?

a few reasons this actually matters

🚫

One wrong item ruins the whole load

A single greasy pizza box or loose plastic bag can contaminate an entire truckload of recycling, sending all of it to the landfill instead of just that one item and that just increases our carbon footprint.

🔁

Glass and aluminum are basically immortal

Melt them down, remake them, repeat — forever, with zero quality loss. Paper and plastic can only be recycled a handful of times before the fibers and polymers degrade too much. Hence making sure that the metal and glass reaches the recycling station is lowkey our responsibly.

⚡

Recycling skips the hard part

Mining, drilling, and refining raw materials from scratch takes way more energy than reprocessing something that already exists.

what each bin is actually for

♻️ Recycling

Purpose: Breaks materials like glass, metal, paper, and certain plastics down so they can become raw material for new products instead of using fresh resources.

Why it matters: It only works if what goes in is the right material and reasonably clean. One contaminated item can spoil an entire batch, which is why sorting carefully actually counts.

🍂 Compost

Purpose: Lets organic material — food scraps and some paper — break down naturally into nutrient-rich soil instead of just rotting away as waste.

Why it matters: Food waste buried in a landfill decomposes without oxygen and produces methane, a potent greenhouse gas. Composting lets it break down aerobically and actually produces something useful.

🗑️ Trash

Purpose: The catch-all for anything that can't be responsibly recycled or composted with current technology, like mixed materials or certain coated papers.

Why it matters: Sending something here on purpose — instead of guessing and tossing it in recycling — actually protects the recycling stream from contamination. A confident "trash" beats a hopeful "maybe recycling."

🔌 Special drop-off

Purpose: Separate handling for items that are hazardous or contain materials worth recovering, like batteries, electronics, and light bulbs.

Why it matters: These items can spark fires, leak toxic materials, or hold recoverable metals that regular trash and recycling streams simply aren't equipped to deal with safely.

can't find it here? go by these rules

works for most stuff that's not in the lists above

🧼

Empty & rinse

Food or liquid left inside a container is the #1 reason recyclables get rejected.

🙅

No "wishcycling"

Tossing something in recycling hoping it'll be fine actually risks contaminating the whole batch. When unsure, look it up.

📏

Size matters

Anything smaller than a credit card (twist ties, bottle caps loose) usually falls through sorting machines. Keep small stuff attached to something bigger, or trash it.

🔌

Batteries & electronics never go loose

Anything with a battery, cord, or plug needs a special e-waste or battery drop-off, never the regular bin.

quick questions

stuff people usually wonder about

Do I need to peel labels off bottles and jars?

Nope, most facilities remove labels during processing. Just make sure the container itself is empty and rinsed.

Can I recycle something even if it's a little dirty?

A light smudge is usually fine, but anything greasy, saucy, or caked-on food can contaminate a whole batch of recycling. When in doubt, give it a quick rinse.

What if my item isn't listed here at all?

Check the "general rules of thumb" section above, or look up your specific city's recycling guidelines — rules can vary a fair bit depending on where you live.
